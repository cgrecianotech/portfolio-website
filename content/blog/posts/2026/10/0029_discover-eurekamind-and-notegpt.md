One thing I haven't quite yet tried out enough in this era of AI is voice workflows. I see lots of people online who interact with their LLMs by just pressing a key and talking to the computer, yet to this day I still always type with my keyboard to prompt my LLMs. I do use Siri on my Apple Watch when I'm out and about to remind me of things, but that's the extent of my voice workflows in my day to day life. So I was quite happy when a NoteGPT representative reached out to me and offered to send a device to try out voice workflows. This also marks the very first time I'm publishing a sponsored YouTube video, which feels like a small milestone in my content creation. Let's take a look at what I will be trying in the next few months:

## NoteGPT
<img src="{{ '0029_notegpt-screenshot.png' | blogImagePath }}" alt="A screenshot of NoteGPT's offering" eleventy:ignore>

First we have [NoteGPT](https://notegpt.io/), which looks like the familiar AI chat interfaces that we're very used to at this point. It has an offering of specialized tasks and flows, aimed at students, educators, and content creators. For example: YouTube video summarizer, AI PDF translator, AI headshot generator... Most generic use AI chat interfaces probably already offer most of these things at this point too, but with a NoteGPT subscription you can also change the underlying model from different providers (ChatGPT, Claude, DeepSeek...).

## EurekaMind 2.0 by Bizcard Limited
<img src="{{ '0029_eurekemind-screenshot.png' | blogImagePath }}" alt="A screenshot of EurekaMind's website" eleventy:ignore>

Then we have [EurekaMind 2.0](https://eurekamind.ai/), which is a pocket-size hardware recorder (~$150). While most AI voice tools require you to have conventional smartphones or smart devices, the folks at Bizcard Limited designed an independent device, the EurekaMind, to capture and process your voice. You can choose to carry both your smartphone and EurekaMind together (the product includes a magnet to attach both products together), but in my opinion the advantage of such a product is that you *don't* need to use your smartphone: you just activate the device and let it record, no matter where you are. I know one of the things I appreciate of my Apple Watch is that I can go outside without my iPhone (maybe to run) and still use Siri to capture thoughts, so this might also go along those lines.

<img src="{{ '0029_eurekamind-attached-iphone.png' | blogImagePath }}" alt="My EurekaMind device attached to my iPhone" eleventy:ignore>

When convenient, you can bring the EurekaMind close to a computer or smartphone and check the data it recorded of your conversations or monologues via their official app. EurekaMind can do a lot of intelligent processing of your voice data once if you configure it to (summaries, action bullet points...).
